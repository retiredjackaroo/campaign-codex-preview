_Chapter 11, Side Quest, Session 7_

## The Wall Comes Down

Evening had settled over the [[Moon Plaza]]. The [[Moon Deva]] hovered cross-legged beside the fountain, a dome of invisible force sealed the lunar mechanism beneath it, and above them all the [[Moonstone]] turned slowly in the air, gathering light.

[[Morwyn Sabbath]] tried first. His Dissonant Whispers found the deva, and it winced at seven points of psychic discord, but the wall held. [[Herschel Blackroot]] reached past Myth Drannor's unstable magic and cast Dispel Magic straight at the dome. It glimmered and stayed where it was.

Herschel lowered his hands and tried words instead. “Your radiance, we're on the same side here.”

The deva's voice came from everywhere at once, echoing and inhuman. “The city has not judged you.”

Into this came [[Mar]] and [[Ripper]], who had woken refreshed from their long, long rest just outside the plaza and followed the sounds of battle. Mar galloped in with Ripper on her back. Ripper vaulted off like a circus acrobat, and Mar shifted into her starry archer form mid-stride. Her luminous arrow went wide and struck the wall of force, but the starry wisps that followed burst across the deva's body in a blaze of radiance and lightning. The celestial flinched, though its swirling form seemed to swallow much of the blast.

Then [[Pug DAstibar|Pug D'Astibar]] sent his spectral companion forward and loosed Magic Missile through it. Four darts struck the deva one after another, each one testing its focus. On the last, a flash of radiance rippled through the plaza and the Wall of Force vanished.

The Moonstone answered at once. It stopped spinning and loosed a blast of radiant lightning. [[Ocreata]] took the full force of it, Morwyn was caught at the edge, and [[Nick Timoney]], still circling low in search of discs, was blown out of the sky.

## A Guardian That Does Not Kill

Ripper did not know what the crystal was, what the deva was, or why everyone was running around looking at the ground. She knew how to fight. She called up the Wrath of the Wild, and her body twisted into something horned and monstrous. The deva, hovering in its meditative pose, seemed entirely unimpressed. Her thrown dagger stuck in its radiant flesh anyway, and her short sword opened a second wound before a column of rushing water crashed down from the deva's hand and swept her twenty feet back across the flagstones.

The pattern soon became clear. The deva did not strike to kill. It pushed Herschel off his feet into a bush. It shoved Ocreata aside. It bound Morwyn, then Mar, in ribbons of liquid moonlight. It raised an earthen hand to grasp at Mar and, later, a gust of wind that turned half the plaza into a wall of pressure. Nearly all the damage came from the Moonstone, which turned, stopped and fired whenever someone wandered into range.

The deva fixed Pug with a long, unblinking look. “You have not been chosen as worthy.”

“Stop speaking in riddles,” said Pug.

Herschel tried again, standing close enough to be heard over the crystal's hum. “Great deva. We do not wish to be harmed, nor to harm you. How can we be judged worthy?”

“I only harm the unworthy,” the deva replied. “You have not been judged. The city will judge you.”

## Discs in the Rubble

While the fighting went on, the escort searched. It was slow, frustrating work: plenty of stones in the ruined plaza were round, but few were polished, threaded and marked with the moon.

Morwyn found the first, half dark and half pale, lying in a heap of broken stone. He Misty Stepped away with it and handed it off to Mar like a rugby ball. Mar, a druid of the stars, knew the sky too well to guess. The disc was the first quarter, and it belonged one socket away from the new moon. It screwed perfectly into place.

Pug found its opposite on his hands and knees between two broken pillars, then pointed Ocreata towards it with all the dignity of a man throwing a stick for a dog. Ocreata had seen the moon symbols all over the city for seven hundred years. He set the third quarter directly opposite Mar's disc.

The Moonstone blasted Pug for that. Pug reached for Absorb Elements, the city's wild magic twisted the spell, and he took the lightning full in the chest while the protective ward settled neatly over the empty socket he had been pointing at. Ocreata let out a high-pitched yelp and covered his face.

“Well done, Pug,” Pug told himself. “Good job. I've protected a stone.”

Morwyn, bound in moonlight, reasoned that he was teleporting himself and not the ribbons, and Misty Stepped cleanly out of them. The DM checked the rules and conceded the point. Morwyn used the freedom to find another disc, the waning crescent. Pug found the waxing gibbous in the shrubs at the foot of a broken arch. Herschel, who had given up on speed, turned into a riding horse and galloped to the far corner by the temple steps, where he found nothing and stamped a hoof in frustration.

## The Sinkhole

The deva tired of Pug's searching. The ground beneath him gave way into a ten-foot sinkhole, and a crushing gravity pinned him to its floor, unable to move or act.

The escort rallied around him. Morwyn tried Dimension Door for the first time in his life and got only a shimmer of arcane light for his trouble. He limped to the fountain instead, set the waning crescent into its place and drank a healing potion. Herschel reared up in horse form and hammered the deva with his hooves, adding a crack of primal thunder, but it held its focus and yanked him thirty feet back across the plaza. Mar, still bound in moonlight, spoke a Healing Word down into the pit. Ocreata threw javelins, drew blood and then spent his breath catching a second wind.

Morwyn cast Mass Healing Word over all six of them. Ocreata freed Mar from the ribbons with brute strength and a strong opinion about knots, while Mar used every ounce of willpower she had not to donkey-kick him.

## What the City Wants

When Ocreata turned his javelins on it, the deva spoke again. “Why do you waste your energy on this? You have not been judged.”

“Where do we get judged?”

“The city judges you.” Even if they defeated the ward, it said, they would not gain entry. The temple was barred to them. “The crystal is a defensive ward protecting the temple. I am the barrier to entry.” To be judged worthy, the city must find them a friend, an ally. They would need to honour its memory, and its history.

Pug thought about the visions he kept seeing and made the connection. Every memory had come from the same era: the coronation of the third and final Coronal, the height of Myth Drannor's golden age. Under that Coronal the prophecy came true. The fiends imprisoned beneath the city returned, gates opened to Avernus and the Abyss, and armies of demons and devils overwhelmed Myth Drannor. The few who lived through it, Elminster among them, rarely speak of it.

Ocreata, prompted at last to share what he knew, explained what the others had missed. The city wants someone who will honour its memory with the reverence it deserves, and it tests people so that it can judge them. Kjeorn's sacrifice before the Binder had been one of those tests. But the city loves to do things in threes. More trials lay ahead, in other places. The deva, he added, was not the city's judge but a manifestation of the Mythal itself. The Mythal was damaged but still working, and that damage explained the wild magic and why Pug's memories came only in fleeting glimpses. The Mythal was what still held the living memory of the city.

Pug and Ocreata both knew where a city like this would keep its duty: the Noble Quarter. In Myth Drannor, nobility meant service. Magistrates held court there, guilds met there, and the archives kept the city's records. Herschel made his position plain: he was here to bleed, not to read.

The escort decided to finish the ward before leaving. Herschel asked the deva to let them neutralise the crystal and go. It answered that it was bound to protect the city. Mar asked to be released so she could leave. “You are trying to disable one of the last defences protecting the most sacred place in this city,” the deva told her. If she left, it would neither help nor hinder her.

## Well Played

The deva released Pug from the sinkhole only to fill the plaza with a roaring gust of wind. Ripper finally found the waxing crescent. Mar spotted the waning gibbous close to the fountain and set it in the last empty socket on the waning side. Ocreata charged the deva at an angle and landed a critical blow, then stood close enough to keep its attention while the others worked.

Pug still held the waxing gibbous, and the gust stood between him and the fountain. He ran straight into it and let it take him. The wind lifted him off his feet and hurled him twenty feet across the plaza. As he sailed over the fountain he dropped the disc, and it fell neatly into its socket.

“Well played,” said the deva.

The Moonstone blasted Ripper one last time. She shook it off, forced her way through the gale on sheer strength, and screwed the waxing crescent into the final socket.

Every socket around the fountain lit with moonlight. The Moonstone stopped spinning, sank into its mount on top of the fountain, and went dark.

## The Last Defence

The deva addressed them all.

“You have deactivated the last defence this city holds against entry into its most sacred space. I am all that is left. I warn you: do not try to enter the temple, or I will be forced to destroy you. You have left me in a weakened state. All of the fiends and creatures of this city now need only get past me to gain entry. If your intention is to be proven worthy, go, and be quick about it.”

![[Campaigns/Moonsea/Assets/Moon Deva.webp]]

> [!caption] The last guardian
> Weakened but unbowed, the Moon Deva warned the escort away from the temple.

Morwyn looked at the weakened guardian and saw a shortcut. He mentioned, too, that it would make a marvellous chapter in his eventual publication.

Ocreata did not laugh. The city had judged _him_ unworthy, he reminded them, and he had spent seven hundred years unable to leave and unable to die. He did not think it was a good idea. Herschel agreed: as friends of the forest and of Myth Drannor, they should respect the deva and play the city's game. Pug decided it for them. Killing the deva would not help, because it was only the Mythal's voice. The city itself would do the judging, and no one knew what waited beyond the temple doors for anyone who entered unjudged.

Ocreata offered a gentler plan. They would take one disc back out of the fountain, so the Moonstone would wake again and guard the temple while they were gone, and carry it with them until they had proved themselves. He volunteered to take the hit when the crystal woke. Whether the city's machinery would allow it, they would find out next time.

## Loose Ends

- All eight lunar discs are fitted and the Moonstone is dormant. The weakened Moon Deva is now the temple's only guardian.
- The escort plans to remove one disc to reactivate the ward while they are away, with Ocreata taking the risk. This has not yet been attempted.
- The city judges through the Mythal. More trials await in other parts of Myth Drannor before Pug can be found worthy to inherit its memory.
- The Noble Quarter, once the heart of Myth Drannor's civil service and archives, is the likeliest place to begin.
- Kjeorn remains bound to the Binder, and the six [[Crystal Vials]] remain empty.
